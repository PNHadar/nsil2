Mass General Brigham Neuroscience Institute

Translating Clinical and Scientific Neuroscience into Venture-Ready Companies

The NSI Launchpad is an incubator-accelerator pathway within the Mass General Brigham Neuroscience Institute, developed in partnership with MGB Innovation. It provides faculty, clinicians, trainees, and staff across Massachusetts General Hospital and Brigham and Women’s Hospital with a structured route from early discovery to company formation and investment readiness.

Mission & Program Design

A Neuroscience-Specific Pathway from Insight to Venture

Translating breakthroughs in neurological and neurosurgical care requires specialized regulatory, clinical, and engineering expertise. The Launchpad combines four core operational components to bridge academic discovery and venture execution.

01

Neuroscience Expertise

Scientific, clinical, and translational guidance rooted in the Mass General Brigham neurology, neurosurgery, and neuroscience community.

02

Structured Programming

A focused weekly curriculum addressing the key clinical, regulatory, intellectual property, and commercial decisions involved in building a venture.

03

Company Building

Hands-on company-formation guidance, team development, customer and clinical discovery, and preparation for seed or Series A financing.

04

Mentors & Connections

Direct access to experienced life-sciences and medtech entrepreneurs, business-school collaborators, industry leaders, and early-stage investors.

Institutional Alignment: The Neuroscience Institute (NSI) provides clinical neuroscience leadership and domain focus, while the NSI Launchpad—working in close partnership with MGB Innovation—provides dedicated venture-building curriculum, IP coordination, and commercialization infrastructure.

Program Architecture

Two-Stage Commercialization Pathway

The Launchpad is structured into two distinct stages so that investigators can enter either with an early clinical or laboratory concept (Stage 1) or with a developing venture that has already demonstrated initial technical or IP progress (Stage 2).

Stage 1 · Tier 1 Pathway

Stage 1 Incubator

A structured launch program for early neuroscience company ideas

3–5 Teams Selected for the inaugural cohort
~12 Weeks One structured session each week

Eligibility & Entry Criteria

Any Mass General Brigham employee may lead a team with a plausible neuroscience company idea. No prior intellectual property, prototype, or existing company is required. Team members and co-founders may come from outside MGB.

Program Support

  • Weekly curriculum covering clinical translation, market validation, IP strategy, and regulatory pathways
  • Access to the NSI Launchpad Mentor Network
  • Company-formation and founding-team guidance
  • Curated introductions to domain, technical, and business experts
  • Pitch preparation culminating in Demo Day
Expected Outcome: Teams graduate ready to form—or having formed—a company, with each selected team receiving a non-dilutive prize award.
Stage 2 · Tier 2 Pathway

Stage 2 Accelerator

Individualized support for ventures that have already shown progress

Up to 3 Ventures In the inaugural accelerator cycle
~6 Months Individualized venture acceleration

Eligibility & Entry Criteria

Both Stage 1 Incubator graduates and independently recruited MGB neuroscience ventures may apply. Applicant teams should demonstrate concrete progress toward a company, product prototype, or protected intellectual property.

Accelerator Support & SAFE Investment

  • Tailored 6-month commercialization and milestone guidance
  • Strategic introductions to venture investors, industry partners, and executive talent
  • Seed and Series A financing preparation and data-room readiness
  • Dedicated Investor Pitch Day with biotech, medtech, and digital health investors
  • SAFE-Based Investment: Structured via a Simple Agreement for Future Equity (SAFE) in coordination with MGB Innovation so NSI shares in long-term upside while maintaining disciplined per-team capital deployment
Expected Outcome: Ventures complete the program positioned for external institutional seed or Series A financing and strategic partnerships.

Strategic Oversight

NSI Launchpad Advisory Board

Comprises approximately eight senior members from outside academia—bringing expertise in venture capital, company formation, legal and IP strategy, and strategic industry partnerships—alongside representation from MGB Innovation. The Advisory Board convenes quarterly to guide program strategy and venture selection.

Operational Guidance

Launchpad Mentor Network

A dedicated network of founders, biotech and medical-device operators, regulatory specialists, and business-school collaborators who work directly with participating Stage 1 and Stage 2 teams on day-to-day company building and commercialization milestones.

Director · Neurology

Sydney S. Cash, MD, PhD

Chief, Division of Epilepsy & Clinical Neurophysiology, Mass General Brigham · Professor of Neurology, Harvard Medical School · Co-Director, Center for Neurotechnology and Neurorecovery (CNTR), MGH

Translational & Entrepreneurial Background: Dr. Cash is a Co-Principal Investigator in the multi-institutional BrainGate intracortical brain-computer interface (BCI) clinical consortium, advancing implantable neural interfaces that restore communication and motor function in patients with paralysis and ALS. At MGH, he co-directs the Center for Neurotechnology and Neurorecovery (CNTR) and the Neurotechnology Trials Unit, and holds multiple patents spanning spatiotemporal seizure prediction algorithms, high-density microelectrode recording arrays (μECoG and human Neuropixels), closed-loop neurostimulation, and implantable neural telemetry.

Academic & Clinical Record: Dr. Cash earned his MD and PhD in Neurobiology from Columbia University and completed his Neurology residency, Chief residency, and Epilepsy/EEG fellowship at Massachusetts General Hospital and Brigham and Women’s Hospital. He serves as Founding Director of the NeuroHINTS Labs and Professor of Neurology at Harvard Medical School, and has authored more than 200 peer-reviewed publications in Science, Nature, Nature Neuroscience, Nature Medicine, and Brain supported by NIH/NINDS R01, U01, and K24 awards.

For Philanthropic Partners

Philanthropic Support & Program Naming

Traditional research grants rarely fund company formation, regulatory preparation, or prototype engineering. Philanthropic support enables MGB discoveries to bridge early translational gaps and attract follow-on venture investment.

  • Stage 1 Prizes & Cohort Support: Underwrite non-dilutive team awards, curriculum delivery, and annual Demo Day.
  • Stage 2 Venture Pool: Support Stage 2 SAFE investments where long-term returns help sustain future MGB neuroscience ventures.
  • Disease-Focused Innovation: Direct support toward ALS, epilepsy, neuro-oncology, stroke, neurocritical care, or neurodegeneration.
